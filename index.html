Sustainability and Civic Engagement

Solidarity trip in Bolivia

Two years ago, I went on a volunteer trip to Bolivia. The goal of this community project was to meet the local populations and support them financially in building a school in a small village deep in the Amazon rainforest. Thanks to this solidarity trip, I was able to spend three weeks completely disconnected — without any network — close to nature and a simple life.

Project about Space debris and sustainable space

In our second year of preparatory school, we carried out a project with five classmates on space debris in order to assess its impact on our world. To do this, we visited CNES and Supaero to interview engineers and researchers working on space debris. Through this project, we became aware of the current challenges in the space industry, learned about methods for recovering certain debris, and discovered initiatives aimed at making space more sustainable and reducing emissions!

Career Development

My academic background has provided me with strong scientific rigor and an excellent ability to analyze complex systems. However, throughout my studies, I realized that my true driving force is not electronic innovation itself, but the need to provide concrete answers to the climate emergency.

I am deeply concerned by environmental issues. This became obvious to me during a solidarity trip to Bolivia (detailed in the "Sustainability" section), where I realized that the populations polluting the least are the most impacted. The ecological and social transition courses I attended in preparatory school also helped me grasp the scale of the problem through data and concrete examples.

That is why I want these themes to be at the core of my future career, rather than just a secondary skill.

This reflection led me to pursue a dual degree in environmental engineering. It represents the perfect springboard for me to fully pivot towards the ecological transition sector, building upon my engineering background.

To help me define precisely what I want to do in this field, I interviewed professionals from my network (on My Job Glasses) who work in sustainable energy.

One of them was a consultant supporting companies in their ecological transition in Canada. This interview was very helpful because it perfectly represents the values I want in my future job: autonomy, human connection, sustainability, and international work.

The second person had approximately the same job, but in France, helping with the development of overseas departments.
